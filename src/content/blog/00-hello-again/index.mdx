It’s now been 10 years since I became a full-time developer. I’ve seen frameworks come and go. I was very lucky to jump on the React bandwagon quite early, and in my free time I grew to love Next.js.

What felt like the new kid on the block that I happily worked on in my spare time is now a multi-billion-dollar company that I include in every slide I present to clients. It’s funny how things develop in life.

Anyway, for this blog I’ve decided to go with something new. Colleagues and friends have been talking about Astro for a while now, and I’ve always felt too busy to look at anything else. To be honest, Next.js has grown into something so big that it’s probably overkill for most basic websites nowadays, so Astro felt like the right tool to give this a go.

I’ve always been a big fan of the KISS principle. If something is too complex, or has too many options for what it was actually meant to do, you eventually create so much friction that your product stops being used for its original purpose, even by your most devoted fans. Every previous version of this blog died that way: I’d spend the evening fiddling with the setup instead of writing anything.

So, let’s see how long this one lasts. Maybe if, for once, the technology doesn’t get in the way, I’ll actually keep journaling this time.
